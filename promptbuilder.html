R-T-C-F example

Role: Act as a friendly instructional designer.

Task: Rewrite this announcement so it is clearer for online students.

Context: Students are in Week 1 and may be new to Brightspace.

Format: Use a short heading, two brief paragraphs, and a 3-item checklist.

CO-STAR+ example

Context: Our college is launching a faculty workshop on AI literacy.

Objective: Write a promotional email that encourages faculty to register.

Style: Clear, practical, and human.

Tone: Encouraging and not salesy.

Audience: Faculty who are curious about AI.

Response: Subject line, 180-word email, and 3 bullets. Avoid jargon.

How to use it

  1. Choose the framework that matches your task.
  2. Select the AI tool and output type.
  3. Answer the guided questions.
  4. Review the generated prompt and quality checks.
  5. Click Copy prompt and paste it into your AI tool.
  6. Iterate by changing one thing at a time.
Best habit: Your first prompt is a draft. Improve it through follow-up requests.

Which framework?

  • R-T-C-F: Everyday text tasks.
  • CO-STAR+: Polished public-facing text.
  • S-S-S + Size: Quick image prompts.
  • SSCL-CM: Detailed, higher-quality visuals.
Image tip: Describe what the camera would see and put the most important detail first.

Platform tips

  • ChatGPT: Great for natural back-and-forth refinement.
  • Claude: Likes clearly labeled sections.
  • Gemini: Good for concise prompts and Google Workspace contexts.
  • Ideogram: Strong for image text and typography.

Before publishing

  • Verify factual claims.
  • Revise the voice so it sounds human.
  • Check audience, tone, and purpose.
  • For images, inspect text, hands, faces, and logos.
